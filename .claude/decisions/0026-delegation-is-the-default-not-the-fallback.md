# OD-0026: Delegation is the default; inline work is the exception that needs a reason
Status: accepted        Date: 2026-08-14
Scope: `CLAUDE.md` orchestrator mandate, do-then-report list (subagent dispatch bullet)
Context: Granted by the owner directly, 2026-08-14, unprompted and in his own words: subagents may be called whenever needed, he wants them used wherever possible to keep the top-level chat clean and minimize token use, and he asked for it in the mandate. The portfolio-wide standing grant (GD-0004, 2026-07-29) already authorized dispatch at Ada's discretion, but authorization is not a preference: the mandate's previous wording ("dispatch freely") permitted delegation without ever making it the expected shape of the work, and the observable result was heavy inline reading and searching in the top-level session.
Decision: "Delegate anything delegable. Reading, searching, auditing, running checks, mechanical edits, and any task whose output is bulky go to a subagent. Keep inline only judgment that cannot be dispatched, and report the conclusion rather than the material it came from. Absorbing delegable work inline is a failure of this rule. If dispatch is blocked, name it in that turn and stop the delegated portion instead of doing it inline."
Consequences: This raises "may dispatch" to "should dispatch" and shifts the burden: inline work now needs a reason, where before delegation did. It composes with the existing verification duty rather than relaxing it, since a subagent's report stays a claim until checked on disk, and with the operating-discipline rule that a frontier tier is never fanned out. The two stated reasons are the owner's own and are recorded here because a mandate carries the imperative, not its provenance.
Disconfirming evidence: The owner reports that dispatch summaries are costing him context he wanted, or that verifying returned diffs has turned into rewriting them, which would mean the specs being sent out are too loose and the split needs re-drawing rather than more delegation.
