Who It's For & Use Cases

Audiobook Studio is for anyone producing long-form spoken audio locally — indie authors, narrators, hobbyists, and developers who want to build on top of it.

At a glance
  • Built for whole-book production, not one-off clips.
  • Iterate freely — fixing a line costs nothing to regenerate.
  • Handles multi-character dialogue with distinct voices.
  • Doubles as a local TTS backend you can automate.

Who it's for

  • Indie authors & self-publishers. Produce an audiobook of your own work at home, no studio time or per-word fees.
  • Narrators & voice artists. Build and reuse voices, scale narration, keep audio on your own machine.
  • Hobbyists & accessibility users. Turn fan fiction, web serials, notes, or documents into audio.
  • Developers & integrators. Use the gateway API, write engine plugins, publish voices.

What it's built to do

Full-book production

The whole point is the long haul. Chapters, characters, a queue that survives restarts, and assembly into a finished file.

Iterative correction without per-edit cost

Revising is free. Because the default engine runs locally, re-record a clumsy line as many times as you like. Change the text, regenerate that segment, move on.

Multi-character dialogue

Every character can sound like themselves. See Characters & Narrators for casting.

Studio as a TTS backend

It's also a local speech engine for your own tools. Through the gateway API, scripts and apps generate speech via the engines you've installed.

Coming with 2.0 — the documented gateway API and installable engines make automation first-class. See TTS Gateway API.

When it might not fit

Studio is a production tool. For a single short clip with no editing, a simpler utility may be faster. Studio shines when there's a book's worth of work to manage.
